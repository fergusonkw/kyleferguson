Section heading

A standard paragraph. Use strong for emphasis that should carry weight on the page, and italics for softer asides or defined terms. Links look like this. Keep paragraphs to a few sentences for readability in print.

A second paragraph follows naturally. This template is tuned for save-to-PDF: margins, page breaks, and the edge-to-edge title block are all handled by the print stylesheet, so what you see on screen is close to what prints.

A sub-heading

Sub-headings (h3) organize detail within a section without the heavier rule of a main heading.

Unordered list

  • First point — a lead-in phrase in bold, then the detail.
  • A second point.
    • A nested sub-point.
    • Another nested sub-point.
  • A third point.

Ordered list

  1. First step in sequence.
  2. Second step in sequence.
  3. Third step in sequence.

Numbered steps (emphasized)

For a more prominent sequence — e.g. "next steps" — use <ol class="steps">. Wrap each item's content in a <div>.

  1. Confirm the approach. A short description of the action and why it matters.
  2. Gather what's needed. Supporting bullets work inside a step too:
    • One requirement.
    • Another requirement.
  3. Schedule the work. The final action.

Wells

A well is a neutral panel for grouping supporting content — assumptions, summaries, scope notes. It can hold a heading, paragraphs, and lists.

Assumptions

  • The first assumption this document depends on.
  • A second assumption.

Summary
